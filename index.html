The map

Beat one

Day one. You won them.
Now what?

You have a handshake, a rough idea, and their enthusiasm. You have no written scope, no order of work, and no list of what you need from them. Everything that goes wrong later starts here.

1 · You won them

Tonight's worked example

Marlowe & Sons. Same deal, one client, all the way through.

"We need a website. And get us on Instagram."

George Marlowe, on the phone, the day he said yes.
  • Four people, thirty seven years, landscaping in Somerset
  • A website and an Instagram. The same two things as the post
  • No photographs, no prices, no phone number he has decided on
  • Nothing written down. A handshake, and a client who is keen

Where it ends up. Every slide from here is this one client: scoped, roadmapped, built, chased, delivered.

1 · You won them

Beat two

"I need a website"
is not a job.

It is a wish. A job is a list of outcomes they can nod at. If you cannot write that list, you do not yet know what you sold, and that is the finding, not a delay.

George gave us nine words.
Page one of his packet turned them into six outcomes he could nod at.

2 · Get clear

Beat two, done

Outcomes, in their words

Not "responsive design". Say "it works properly on a phone, because that is where most people will see it". They can only agree to a sentence they understand.

Marlowe & Sons, page one of their packet.

2 · Get clear

Beat three

The section everybody skips.

Every unpaid job you have ever been dragged into was something nobody wrote down on day one. "While you're in there, could you just..." is not a request. It is the absence of a boundary.

3 · Scope creep

And this is the move

You are not saying no.
You are drawing the line.

"Set up" stays in. "Run it" comes out. Same deliverable, with an edge on it. Say it once, in writing, on day one, and the conversation three weeks from now is thirty seconds instead of an argument.

The line does not go around the deliverable.
It goes through it.

3 · Scope creep

Beat three, done

Write down what is not in it

In the same breath as what is. Not as a refusal, as a boundary. Then say out loud that any of them can be added as separate work, quoted separately, once this is live.

Marlowe & Sons again. The line runs through the Instagram job, not around it.

3 · Scope creep

Beat four

One document. These six headings, every time.

1What we agreedthe job, in their words
4Built and workingproof you are moving
2What is not in thisthe line, drawn early
5Waiting on youranked, not listed
3The roadmaporder, and what blocks what
6What happens nextthe date of the next one

Not an email. Not a chat message they scroll past. A document with their name on it.

4 · The document

And it is the same document every time

You write it once. You regenerate it forever.

Day one

A scope agreement

What we agreed, what is not in it, the phases. Sent before any code.

Week three

A status report

What is built, what is still waiting on them, which phase is blocked.

The end

The handover

Everything delivered, everything settled, nothing left to argue about.

Same six headings every time, so they can see exactly what moved since the last one.

4 · The document

Beat five

The roadmap you build against

Three to five phases. Each one an outcome they understand, not a task list. And each one declares what it needs before it can start.

5 · The roadmap

Why the roadmap earns its place

A blocked phase stops
everything behind it.

This is the sentence that gets a client to act. Not "please send the photos". Say "phase three and phase four of your site are both sitting still until they arrive, and your Instagram cannot open without them", and show him the phases sitting still.

5 · The roadmap

Marlowe & Sons, phase three

This is what "waiting on you" actually looks like

The site is finished and it cannot go live. Not because of code. Because six photographs never arrived.

5 · The roadmap

So which one do they do first?

Rank by what it unblocks. Then print the number.

Send the photographs
→
Phase 3 · his real content can start
Phase 4 · going live properly can start
his Google Business Profile goes up
the first nine Instagram posts go out

So the packet says, in their language:
"This one unblocks 4 other things."

5 · The roadmap

The page that actually unblocks projects

The shot list

"Send some photos" gets nothing. Tell a non photographer exactly what to stand where and photograph, on a phone, and it arrives. This is the list that fills his gallery and seeds his Instagram grid.

  • The walled garden at Croscombe, wide, whole wall in frame
  • The same garden close up, stonework readable
  • The terrace and steps at Wells, from the house looking out
  • A dry stone wall you repaired, flat or cloudy light
  • The team mid job, anyone, not posed
  • One before and one after, same spot
5 · The roadmap

Beat six

Do the first one by hand.

Open the project. Look at what actually changed since last week. Tick off what is genuinely done, move what is still missing to the top, write the three lines that say where you are, and send it.

Ten minutes per client, per week.
Fine for two clients. Impossible at ten.

6 · Check in

So hand that part over

It ticks itself off by
reading the code.

A client will tell you the photographs are sent. The repo tells you whether they arrived. The check-in reads the project the same way you just did by hand, and never marks something done because somebody said so.

And the whole thing in one line

You either front-load the cost of getting clear,
or you pay it every week you work with them.

An hour on day one, or an argument a month for six months. It is the same money. You just choose when you spend it, and whether you get paid for it.
