The map

Beat one

Monday. You won them.
Now what?

You have a handshake, a rough idea, and their enthusiasm. You have no written scope, no order of work, and no list of what you need from them. Everything that goes wrong later starts here.

1 · You won them

Tonight's worked example

Marlowe & Sons, a four person landscaping firm

We will take one client the whole way: won on a Monday, scoped, roadmapped, built, chased, delivered. This is where it ends up.

1 · You won them

Beat two

"I need a website"
is not a job.

It is a wish. A job is a list of outcomes they can nod at. If you cannot write that list, you do not yet know what you sold, and that is the finding, not a delay.

2 · Get clear

Beat two, done

Outcomes, in their words

Not "responsive design". Say "it works properly on a phone, because that is where most people will see it". They can only agree to a sentence they understand.

2 · Get clear

Beat three

The section everybody skips.

Every unpaid job you have ever been dragged into was something nobody wrote down on day one. "While you're in there, could you just..." is not a request. It is the absence of a boundary.

3 · Scope creep

Beat three, done

Write down what is not in it

In the same breath as what is. Not as a refusal, as a boundary. Then say out loud that any of them can be added as separate work, quoted separately, once this is live.

3 · Scope creep

Beat four

One document.
Six headings.

Not an email. Not a chat message they scroll past. A document with their name on it, that says what we agreed, what is excluded, the order of work, what is done, and what you need from them.

4 · The document

And it is the same document every time

You write it once. You regenerate it forever.

Day one

A scope agreement

What we agreed, what is not in it, the phases. Sent before any code.

Week three

A status report

What is built, what is still waiting on them, which phase is blocked.

The end

The handover

Everything delivered, everything settled, nothing left to argue about.

Same six headings every time, so they can see exactly what moved since the last one.

4 · The document

Beat five

The roadmap you build against

Three to five phases. Each one an outcome they understand, not a task list. And each one declares what it needs before it can start.

5 · The roadmap

Why the roadmap earns its place

A blocked phase stops
everything behind it.

This is the sentence that gets a client to act. Not "please send the photos". Say "phase three and phase four are both sitting still until they arrive", and show them the phases sitting still.

5 · The roadmap

So what do they do first?

Rank by what unblocks the most. Then print the number.

Photographs
→
Phase 3
Phase 4
the Google Business Profile
the Instagram grid

A dependency count, not a feeling. Not what is hardest, not what annoys you most.

5 · The roadmap

And this is what blocked looks like

Finished. And it still cannot go live.

Not because of code. Because six photographs never arrived.

5 · The roadmap

The page that actually unblocks projects

The shot list

"Send some photos" gets nothing. Tell a non photographer exactly what to stand where and photograph, on a phone, and it arrives.

  • The walled garden, wide, whole wall in frame
  • The same garden close up, stonework readable
  • A wall you repaired, flat or cloudy light
  • The team mid job, not posed
  • One before and one after, same spot
5 · The roadmap

Beat six

It ticks itself off by
reading the code.

A client will tell you the photographs are sent. The repo tells you whether they arrived. Never mark something done because somebody said so.

6 · Check in

The check-in, every Monday

Re-read the project. Rewrite the document.

> use client-packet — check in on ~/projects/marlowe
5 files scanned.
  landed:    Six photographs of finished gardens
  landed:    The phone number you want on the site
  unblocked: Phase 4 · Go live properly
3 still outstanding. Now version 5, written to packet.html.

Two asks landed, so a phase opened itself and the document rewrote itself. Nobody typed a status update.

6 · Check in

Where it ends up

The document is the deliverable. The loop is the product.

Prompt→ Skill→ Loop→ Routine

Monday morning, one message: every client, which phase they are blocking, and the documents already written.

Three clients becomes ten Without hiring anyone
6 · Check in

If you take nothing else

Two things worth stealing, even if you never run the code

Write down what is not included

On day one, in the same breath as what is. It is the cheapest hour you will ever spend.

Rank by what it unblocks

And print the number in the document. "This one unblocks four other things" is what makes a client act.
